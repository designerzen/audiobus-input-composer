# 25 — Additional papers: contribution, application and evidence limits

These extend [P01–P17](03-annotated-papers.md). PDF text was retrieved for all eight records below; first-page overviews and selected method/evaluation passages were inspected. This is not a full visual review, reproduction or audit of every claim. [The inspection ledger](references/live-coding-paper-inspection.json) records URLs, PDF hashes and page counts. Bibliographic records are appended to the existing [BibTeX file](references/research-bibliography.bib).

## P18 — Strudel: Live Coding Patterns on the Web

Felix Roos and Alex McLean, ICLC 2023. [Paper record](https://zenodo.org/records/7842142).

**Contribution:** a web implementation of a related pattern model, with modular outputs and visual feedback. **Application:** evaluate a browser pattern adapter independently from the composer policy. **Expected effect:** rich temporal transformations without binding the score to one renderer. **Limit:** an implementation/design paper does not establish this Input's musical coherence or remote timing. Read alongside current manuals because APIs and defaults can change.

## P19 — Ziffers: Numbered Musical Notation for Live Coding and Algorithmic Composition

Miika Alonen and Raphaël Maurice Forment, ICLC 2023. [Paper record](https://zenodo.org/records/7841945).

**Contribution:** a concise, implementation-agnostic musical notation bridging fixed and generative material. **Application:** retain degrees, rhythm and transformations in an editable musical program. **Expected effect:** motif variants remain theoretically inspectable and transposable. **Limit:** expressive notation is not a policy for form or autonomous interaction, and hosts need explicit compatibility checks.

## P20 — Estuary: Browser-based Collaborative Projectional Live Coding of Musical Patterns

David Ogborn, Jamie Beverley, Luis Navarro del Angel, Eldad Tsabary and Alex McLean, ICLC 2017. [Paper](https://iclc.toplap.org/2017/cameraReady/ICLC_2017_paper_78.pdf).

**Contribution:** structured editing and collaboration, including explicit treatment of when edits become live. **Application:** offer valid musical actions through the diagram and commit them at declared boundaries. **Expected effect:** fewer malformed edits and more understandable changes. **Limit:** the paper describes an early design, not a guarantee about every current platform feature or network timing condition.

## P21 — Disruption and Creativity in Live Coding

Ushini Attanayake, Ben Swift, Henry Gardner and Andrew Sorensen, VL/HCC 2020. [Author manuscript](https://benswift.me/assets/documents/preprints/attanayake_et_al_2020_disruption_and_creativity_in_live_coding.pdf).

**Contribution:** examines recommendation versus autonomous disruptive interventions. **Application:** compare Suggest, Conduct and Provoke modes rather than equating initiative with usefulness. **Expected effect:** expose the trade-off between exploratory disturbance and control. **Limit:** six participants, situated sessions and a first-position no-agent condition constrain generalization; this is not a validated unattended composition policy.

## P22 — On the Integration of Machine Agents into Live Coding

Elizabeth Wilson, György Fazekas and Geraint Wiggins, *Organised Sound*, 2023. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1355771823000420); [author manuscript](https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/21566/1/on-the-integration-of-machine-agents-into-live-coding.cleaned.pdf).

**Contribution:** articulates reflection, aesthetics and evaluation as concerns in machine collaboration. **Application:** make an agent's authority and evaluative criteria explicit. **Expected effect:** the person can understand and correct the scope of autonomous changes. **Limit:** a conceptual design argument is not evidence that an agent has human-like judgment or improves listener preference.

## P23 — Reproducible Musical Analysis of Live Coding Performances Using Information Retrieval: A Case Study on the Algorave 10th Anniversary

Georgios Diapoulis and Martin Carlé, ICLC 2023. [Author repository copy](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/535526/file/535526_Fulltext.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7843813).

**Contribution:** reproducible analysis across 133 performances. **Application:** evaluate complete musical trajectories and diversity rather than cherry-picked loops. **Expected effect:** diagnose overly similar developmental arcs across seeds. **Limit:** audio-derived descriptors do not directly recover symbolic decisions or establish perceived emotion; an event's repertoire does not represent every Algorave practice.

## P24 — Multi-Agent Swarm Syntax: An Approach to Live Coding with AI

Sven Hollowell, Pete Bennett and Paul Marshall, NIME 2026. [Paper record](https://zenodo.org/records/20784519).

**Contribution:** visible machine editing and concurrent interaction in a Strudel-based environment. **Application:** separate machine participation visibility from musical decision authority. **Expected effect:** more legible intervention and correction. **Limit:** collaborative autoethnography supports situated interaction insights, not a controlled finding of better music, reliable emotion following or universally useful autonomy.

## P25 — Designing for a Pluralist and User-Friendly Live Code Language Ecosystem with Sema

Francisco Bernardo, Chris Kiefer and Thor Magnusson, ICLC 2020. [Paper record](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3939228).

**Contribution:** language design and machine-learning workflows as configurable creative resources. **Application:** design a bounded musical vocabulary around this instrument's needs. **Expected effect:** performers can steer meaningful operations rather than arbitrary code. **Limit:** the reported workshop concerns an early system and learning context; it does not show that non-programmers can use our diagram or that its mappings are effective.

## Synthesis of the evidence

P18–P20 explain representation, execution and editable participation. P21–P22 show why initiative and agency need explicit treatment. P23 motivates performance-scale analysis. P24 adds a contemporary example of visible AI participation. P25 makes language design a deliberate musical choice.

Together they strengthen the case for an inspectable musical program and explicit intervention policy. They leave the hardest project questions open: how a wheel selection should change music, how a conductor should sustain a long arc, and whether users hear coherent expression. Those require the targeted studies in [26](26-research-gaps-and-experiment-programme.md).
